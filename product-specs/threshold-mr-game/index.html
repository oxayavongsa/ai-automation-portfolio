Overview

THRESHOLD is a mixed reality game where your real home is the battlefield: walls crack open, other worlds pour in, and the people you love fight beside you. It blends horror, suspense, action and an emotional core into one replayable hub game for Meta Quest 3 and 3S.

The genre blend:

  • Horror — things move behind your real furniture, lights in the game flicker in sync with spatial audio, and you never know which wall breaks next.
  • Suspense — enemies hunt by sound; whispering and standing still are real tactics.
  • Action — physical combat with your hands: punching, blocking, throwing, aiming, dodging in your own living room.
  • Love — your team can be your real friends and family as AI companions, and the story is about protecting home.

Story and lore

Every home sits on a thin spot in the Veil, the barrier between our world and the Beyond. A failed experiment by the Halcyon Institute tore the Veil, and now breaches open inside ordinary houses. You are a Warden, one of the few people whose home can hold a breach shut.

Factions (each powers a set of levels):

  • The Hollow — restless dead pulled through the Veil. Horror levels.
  • The Kethari — an alien warrior race that settles disputes by hand-to-hand combat. Action and boxing levels.
  • Halcyon — the institute that caused it all, still running experiments and watching Wardens. The hidden villain behind the meta-puzzle.
  • The Kept — friendly echoes of people, living and passed, who choose to stand with you. This is where your uploaded friends and family live in the story.

The emotional spine: your guide is Echo, a voice that knows your home better than you do. Over the seasons players learn Echo is someone who lost their own family to a breach and is trying to make sure you never do. The final reveal is earned only by finding secrets across every level.

Story delivery: short spoken scenes from Echo and companions, environmental clues placed on your real walls (scratched messages, Halcyon files), and audio logs found as secrets. No long cutscenes; players stay in their room and in control.

Core loop and controls

Each session follows one loop that keeps players coming back: scan, breach, survive, discover, return home stronger.

  1. Scan — the game reads your room (walls, doors, couch, table, windows) through MRUK. A saved scan loads in seconds.
  2. Pick a mission — from the Hub, a glowing map projected on your real wall.
  3. Breach — a wall cracks open. The mission plays out in your room plus the world behind the wall.
  4. Survive and win — fight, hide, solve, protect your companions.
  5. Discover — secrets, rare items, lore pieces; most are only visible if you look in the right place at the right time.
  6. Return home — trophies appear on your real shelves, companions level up, new rooms in the story unlock.

Comfort settings: no artificial movement in MR mode, adjustable jump-scare intensity (Low / Normal / Nightmare), seated mode, photosensitivity filter, one-hand mode.

Levels

Launch with one flagship level done extremely well, then add a level every season. Each level has three difficulty tiers and a hidden fourth tier unlocked by secrets.

Level 1 — Night Shift (horror / suspense) — flagship

Your house is haunted by the Hollow. Your real lights stay on, but the game darkens the passthrough view and gives you a flashlight.

  • Goal: find and seal 3 haunted objects before the Hollow Mother fully crosses over.
  • Mechanics: flashlight beam reveals ghosts; an EMP reader buzzes near them; salt lines (drawn with your hand on the floor) block their path; ghosts freeze when you look directly at them and move when you look away.
  • Enemies: Whisperers (hide behind real furniture, steal items), Crawlers (come through the floor), the Hollow Mother (boss who reaches through your real doorway).
  • Set pieces: a closet door that slowly opens on its own; a phone ringing from behind a wall; your companion gets pulled through a crack and you must reach in to pull them back.
  • Suspense rule: the game reads your microphone volume; loud players attract ghosts.

Level 2 — Impact (action / fitness)

The Kethari challenge you to an honor duel. Your ceiling opens to a starship arena.

  • Goal: win a 3-round boxing tournament against escalating alien fighters.
  • Mechanics: real punches (jab, hook, uppercut detected by hand speed and angle), real blocks and ducks, combo multipliers, stamina meter that rewards breathing and rhythm.
  • Fitness layer: calories, punch count and heart-zone estimate after every match; weekly workout streaks.
  • Twist: win enough and the Kethari join you as allies in other levels.

Level 3 — The Den (hangout / social)

A calm mode. Friends appear in your living room as avatars.

  • Mini-games on your real table: cards, air hockey, alien arm wrestling, ghost-story campfire.
  • Show off trophies and rare finds on your shelves.
  • Watch videos together on a virtual screen on your wall.

Level 4 — Legacy (love / memory)

A quiet space for a loved one who has passed. See the Companions section for how it works. No enemies, no timers; fishing, porch talks, playing a favorite card game, hearing their stories in their voice.

Replay rules: enemy positions shuffle every run; 30% of dialogue changes between runs; a Secrets Journal shows silhouettes of what is still missing, never locations; players can post their finds with in-game clip capture.

Co-op roles (optional, swap any time): Seer (flashlight, finds ghosts), Guardian (blocks and protects), Fixer (seals cracks, solves puzzles), Brawler (melee damage).

Safety for teens: friends-only voice by default, mute and block in one gesture, no open voice lobbies with strangers, reporting built in.

Companions, uploads and Legacy

Companions are AI teammates with a face, voice and personality. Players can use built-in characters or create one from a real person who has given consent.

Creation pipeline (runs in the cloud, not on the headset):

  1. Upload 3–10 photos or a 30-second video of the person.
  2. Upload 1–3 minutes of their voice (videos and voicemails work).
  3. Fill in a personality card: nicknames, sayings, likes, stories, how they talk.
  4. The server builds a 3D avatar (photo-to-avatar service or Gaussian splat), clones the voice (ElevenLabs API), and creates a personality profile for the dialogue model.
  5. The companion appears in the game in 5–15 minutes.

In play: companions talk live using a language-model API with their personality card, speak with their cloned voice, react to danger, joke, remember past missions, and can be rescued or lost for a mission (never permanently).

Consent rules (living people):

  • The person in the upload records a short consent selfie video in the companion app; the system matches the face to the uploaded photos.
  • Voice cloning requires the person to read a verification phrase, the same approach ElevenLabs uses.
  • Anyone can revoke consent and the companion is deleted from every account.
  • Faces that appear to be under 18 are blocked from upload.

Legacy mode (loved ones who have passed):

  • The uploader attests they are immediate family or have the family's permission; any relative can request a takedown.
  • Old photos are restored and colorized before the avatar is built.
  • Legacy companions live in a calm space (porch, dock, kitchen table) and only join other levels if the family turns that on.
  • A gentle on-screen note says this is an AI recreation; easy exit at any time.
  • Celebrities and public figures are blocked.

Progression and monetization

Progress comes from skill and discovery, never from paying; money buys looks, levels and companion slots.

  • Warden Rank — XP from missions and secrets; unlocks gear, tools and difficulty tiers.
  • Companion bonds — each companion levels up separately and unlocks new dialogue, abilities and shared memories.
  • Trophy Shelf — physical-looking trophies placed on your real shelves; friends see them in The Den.
  • Fitness log — weekly calories, punches and active minutes from Impact and Rhythm Rift.

Art and audio direction

The look is cinematic stylized realism: believable materials, lighting and motion, tuned to run at 72–90 fps on Quest's mobile chip. True photorealism is not possible on standalone headsets yet; realism here comes from lighting, animation and sound.

  • Visuals: baked lighting for worlds behind the walls, real-time light on your room, volumetric fog through breaches, particle effects for dust and debris when walls crack.
  • Characters: motion-captured animation (rent a mocap suit or use AI video-to-motion tools), detailed faces only on hero characters, simpler models for crowds.
  • Breach effect: the signature moment — walls crumble into chunks with real physics, revealing the world behind.
  • Audio: fully spatial sound (Meta XR Audio SDK). Footsteps behind you, whispers from a specific corner, a knock from inside your closet. Audio is 50% of the horror.
  • Haptics: controller rumble on hits; a heartbeat pulse when a ghost is near.
  • Performance budget: under 300K triangles on screen, under 150 draw calls, foveated rendering on.

Technical architecture

All gameplay runs on the headset; only companion creation and live dialogue need the cloud, so the game stays playable if AI services are down.

Backend options: Unity Gaming Services or a Firebase/Supabase backend. Companion lines are generated ahead of time where possible (reactions, barks) and live only for conversation, to keep latency under 1.5 seconds and cost down.

Build roadmap and budget

Start with a cheap playable prototype to prove the scares and secrets are fun, then spend real money only after people ask to play again.

Gates: move on only when the gate is met. Fun test = most testers want a second run without being asked. Store approval = App Lab accepts the build. Traction = steady weekly players and strong reviews you can show a publisher.

Hiring for the vertical slice (Upwork or Toptal): 1 senior Unity XR developer (MRUK and Shared Spatial Anchors experience, with a shipped Quest title), 1 3D artist and animator, 1 part-time sound designer. You act as producer and game director.

Full vision budget: $1.5M to $3M and a 15 to 25 person team, reached through publisher funding after traction. Publishers to pitch: Fast Travel Games, nDreams, Vertigo Games, and Meta's own content funding programs.
